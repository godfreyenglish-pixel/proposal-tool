Your options, side by side

Tap a card to load it into every chapter above.

The honest bottom line: on your usage, the savings make a battery cheaper to own, but they won't pay it back quickly. The real reason to get one is backup power when the grid goes down. One battery covers your essentials through a typical outage; two stretch to a day or more.

How this was built. Your Con Ed interval data (5,912 kWh, Nov 1 2025 to Oct 31 2026). All-in rates rebuilt from your Sept 15 to Oct 15, 2026 bill and checked against it to the cent ($142.71): supply 12.173¢ plus merchant function charge, Con Ed delivery with 2026 riders, system benefit charge, gross receipts tax and 4% sales tax. Time-of-use: peak weekdays 8 AM to midnight, off-peak midnight to 8 AM and weekends. Battery charges overnight at 90% round-trip efficiency and covers peak use until empty. Supply held the same on both plans. Battery savings reduced 1% a year for capacity fade. Net prices are after the NYSERDA storage incentive. Estimates only; actual savings depend on future rates and how your use changes.
