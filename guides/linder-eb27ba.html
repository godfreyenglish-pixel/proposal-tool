7

Your options, side by side

Tap a card to load it into every chapter above.

The honest bottom line: the reason to get a battery is backup power when the grid goes down. EG4 16 kWh × 1 runs your whole house about 19 hours at average use, and a lot longer on just your essentials. On top of that, switching plans is worth about $176 a year and the battery adds about $373, so the savings make that backup cheaper to own.

How this was built. Your Con Ed interval data (5,912 kWh, Nov 1 2025 to Oct 31 2026). All-in rates rebuilt from your Sep 15 to Oct 15, 2026 bill and checked against it ($142.71): Con Ed supply plus merchant function charge, Con Ed delivery with current riders, system benefit charge, gross receipts tax and sales tax. Time-of-use: peak weekdays 8 AM to midnight, off-peak midnight to 8 AM and weekends. Battery charges overnight at 90% round-trip efficiency and covers peak use until empty. Supply held the same on both plans. Battery savings reduced 1% a year for capacity fade. Net prices are after the NYSERDA storage incentive. Backup hours assume a full battery at the start of the outage and no solar recharging. Estimates only; actual savings depend on future rates and how your use changes.
