How your meter counts

Your panels feed the house first. Con Ed's meter only sees what's left over: power you buy when the panels fall short, and power you send back when they make extra. Sent-back power goes into a credit bank you draw from later.

Two possible causes

Buying more from the grid can mean the panels make less, or the house uses more. The meter can't tell them apart. Tap each card.

For scale

A healthy 10 kW system around here typically makes roughly 10,000 to 12,000 kWh a year (estimate, depends on roof direction and shade). Your Enphase app shows exactly what yours made each month. If it's in that range, the panels are fine and the house grew. If it's well under, we fix that first.

Fixed charges are the $21.20 basic charge plus Con Ed's $13.63 solar charge ($1.363 per kW of solar, billed on your 10 kW), with tax. That solar charge is tied to system size, so an expansion raises it. Before your EAP credit.

Market context, not a quote. Grid power runs about 35¢ a kWh and rises every year (Con Ed's approved plan adds roughly 3 to 4% a year through 2028). A system done right locks in a far lower cost per kWh, fixed for 25 years. The number to compare between any two quotes is cost per kWh over the system's life, plus what the warranty actually covers, labor included.
