2

Something electric is heating this house

December through February used roughly 3,050 kWh, about 35% of the whole year in three months. On the coldest days in late January and early February 2026 your daily use jumped past 80 kWh, right when outdoor temps dropped near single digits.

You mentioned the gas furnace just kicked on. Its blower fan runs on electric, but that's usually a few kWh a day, not the 15 to 20 extra we saw last winter. Electric use that tracks the thermometer this closely usually means one of these is pulling hard:

Space heaters

Two or three running in bedrooms can add 20+ kWh a day.

Heat pump or electric baseboard

Heating part of the house (a finished basement, an addition) on electric.

This matters for sizing. A solar system built only off your summer bills would come up short in the months you use the most.

3

Summer is about AC, and PSE&G charges more for it

July and August ran about 29 to 30 kWh a day, roughly 12 kWh above baseline. That's your cooling.

From June to September PSE&G uses a two-step rate: once you pass 600 kWh in a bill, every extra kWh costs more on both delivery and supply. Your September bill hit 631 kWh, so the last 31 kWh were on the higher step. July and August were well past it.
